# NASA Zooniverse Active Asteroids
## Label Uncertainty in Computer Vision

An independent computer vision research project investigating how **image-level label uncertainty** affects machine learning classification of active asteroid cutouts, using publicly accessible resources from the [NASA Zooniverse Active Asteroids](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/orionnau/active-asteroids) citizen science program.

---

## 1. What is Active Asteroids?

[Active Asteroids](https://www.zooniverse.org/projects/orionnau/active-asteroids) is a NASA Partner Citizen Science program hosted on the Zooniverse platform (Project ID: `6801`). The project invites volunteers to inspect astronomical thumbnail cutouts extracted from the Dark Energy Camera (DECam) archive (Victor M. Blanco 4-meter Telescope in Chile) to detect rare Solar System objects exhibiting comet-like tails or comae despite having asteroid-like orbits (Chandler et al. 2024, *The Astronomical Journal*, 167:156). Identifying active asteroids helps map volatile distributions (such as water ice) across the Solar System and illuminates dynamical processes such as impacts and rotational mass shedding.

---

## 2. The Scientific Problem & Why Label Uncertainty Matters

In standard supervised machine learning, models are trained under the assumption that ground-truth labels accurately reflect the visual content of each training image. 

In asteroid astronomy, however, ground-truth labels are established at the **object level** based on historical discoveries. This creates a critical machine learning problem:
> **An asteroid can be historically classified as active at the object level, while a particular observation may not visibly show activity.**

Active asteroids are often active only intermittently (e.g., near perihelion, or following a short-lived collision or rotational disruption). In dormant orbital phases or poor seeing conditions, an active asteroid appears purely point-source-like (starlike). If a machine learning model is trained or evaluated using binary catalog labels without accounting for this distinction, it is penalized for correctly recognizing the visual absence of a tail. This mismatch represents a severe form of **image-level label uncertainty**.

## 3. Data Provenance & Methodology Disclosures

### Public Sources
- **Metadata Records**: 200 authentic DECam subject metadata records fetched directly via the Zooniverse Panoptes REST API (`/api/subjects`):
  - **Teaching Set (`96361`)**: 100 cutouts curated by astronomers to teach volunteers, featuring comets and active Centaurs (e.g., Chiron, 2014 OG392, Echeclus, C/2010 R1) with prominent visual tails.
  - **General Stream (`36656`)**: 100 archival DECam observations of known minor planets. Cross-matched against a 25-entry catalog of verified active small bodies (Chandler et al. 2024). Contains **16 catalog-matched cutouts** (1 observation of Centaur 2060 Chiron and 15 observations of Main-belt comet 62412 2000 SY178) and **84 unmatched inactive asteroid cutouts** (e.g., 65 Cybele, 704 Interamnia, 3552 Don Quixote).

### Important Technical Audit Disclosures
1. **Procedural Cutout Simulation**: To avoid downloading gigabytes of raw astronomical imagery to a local disk with limited capacity, the model training and evaluation were executed using **procedural synthetic image cutouts** (`generate_synthetic_cutout`) keyed to the verified metadata. While the pipeline and model architectures are fully functional, the metrics reflect performance on this synthetic benchmark rather than raw DECam pixel files.
2. **Asteroid-Identity Leakage**: While splits are strictly leak-free at the `subject_id` level, asteroid-level leakage exists: Centaur (2060) Chiron appears in both `train` and `val`, and 10 inactive asteroid designations appear across multiple splits.
3. **Test Set Active Diversity**: All 8 positive examples in the held-out test set are multi-epoch observations of a single asteroid: (62412) 2000 SY178.

---

## 4. Models Evaluated

1. **Baseline Model**: Logistic Regression (`C=1.0`, balanced class weights) trained on 8 hand-crafted spatial and flux features (central concentration, global contrast, hot-tail spread, central variance, horizontal gradient roughness, Laplacian edge energy, peak-to-background flux, and moment-based elongation).
2. **Deep Learning Model**: ResNet18 fine-tuning (ImageNet pre-trained), modified with a 1-output linear layer and dropout ($p=0.3$), trained using `WeightedRandomSampler`, `BCEWithLogitsLoss`, and `CosineAnnealingLR` over 10 epochs with early stopping on validation PR-AUC.

## 6. Key Scientific Findings & Error Analysis

Across all 100 evaluation predictions (validation and test across both models on the benchmark):
- **Specificity on Inactive Asteroids**: 100% (68 true negatives, 0 false positives).
- **False Negative Rate on Catalog-Matched Positives**: **78.12%** (25 out of 32 catalog-matched active cutouts were classified as inactive at the standard 0.5 threshold).
- **High-Confidence False Negatives ($p < 0.20$)**: 20 out of 32 (62.5%).

### The "High-AUC, Low-Recall" Phenomenon
ResNet18 achieved high ranking separation (Test ROC-AUC = 0.9926), yet missed over 78% of simulated active asteroids when evaluated at the default 0.5 threshold. 

The model's predicted probability for catalog-matched active asteroids averaged **0.2687**—an order of magnitude higher than inactive asteroids (**0.0262**). The classifier *does* learn separation. However, because it was trained on curated high-contrast examples, its output probabilities were miscalibrated for lower-contrast or ambiguous cases, demonstrating the danger of uncalibrated decision thresholds under label uncertainty.
